What does "NeuroBusiness" mean —
and what does this report measure?

Personality tests are everywhere. What sets the NeuroBusiness Intelligence Framework (NBIF) apart: it connects established neuropsychological constructs to concrete business contexts. Not "Who am I?" — but "How do I operate in my business, under pressure, when deciding, in a team?"

The "Neuro" in NBIF

"Neuro" does not refer to brain scans or medical findings. It describes neuropsychological behavior patterns — observable, measurable tendencies in how people process information, make decisions, and respond to stress. These patterns are stable, learnable, and can be deliberately developed.

The 5 dimensions — their basis
D1
Energy & VisibilityIntroversion vs. extraversion · Big Five research (Costa & McCrae), neural activation models
D2
Cognition & Decision StyleAnalytical vs. intuitive · Kahneman (System 1/2), cognitive style research
D3
Regulatory FocusPromotion vs. prevention · Regulatory Focus Theory (Higgins, 1997) — broadly evidenced
D4
Execution StylePlanned vs. spontaneous · Implementation intentions (Gollwitzer), action orientation
D5
Stress RegulationResilience vs. vulnerability · Burnout dimensions (Maslach), stress-coping research
How to read your scores

Scores aren't grades. A score of 82 on D1 doesn't mean "better" than 50 — it shows that your energy processing leans strongly toward the introverted-focused end of the spectrum.

Intensity bands
80–100: Very strongly expressed
60–79: Strongly expressed
40–59: Moderate / balanced
20–39: Opposite pole, strong
0–19: Opposite pole, very strong

Your cognitive style is clearly analytical — you process sequentially, look for causality, and build mental models. Business consequence: why you're undervalued despite your competence

D1 (depth focus) + D2 (analytics) create a depth of knowledge that can't be transferred in short encounters. Others judge quickly — your brain communicates slowly. Your market value often doesn't match your actual capability. That's not a charisma problem. It's a visibility system failure.

06 · Decision-Making Behavior
Cognitive architecture
How the {{TYP_KURZ}}
makes decisions

Your decision process follows a characteristic four-phase pattern — directly derived from D2 (analytical) + D3 (prevention focus) + D4 (structured). No other psychotype has exactly this configuration.

1
Information gathering (above-average length)More data than needed — because your prevention focus (D3) keeps signaling that more certainty is possible.
2
Internal model-buildingMental simulation of every scenario. In hindsight, usually the right call — but expensive in time.
3
Risk simulation (D3 loop)"What could go wrong?" runs automatically. Can lead to decision deadlock when risks stay unquantifiable.
4
Commitment with convictionOnce decided: fully behind it. Strategist decisions are rarely impulsive — and rarely wrong.
DECISION QUALITY
Strategic long-term decisionsExcellent
Complex analytical decisionsVery good
Tactical quick decisionsMedium
Spontaneous impulse decisionsHigh cost
⚠
Analysis paralysis: with incomplete information, timing keeps slipping — while market windows close. Typical of the Strategist D2×D3 combination.
⚠
Sunk-cost resistance: once a plan is set, it's kept even as conditions change — your systems thinking becomes a fixation trap.

Your override mechanism: hard deadline rituals: "I'll decide by Friday noon — with whatever information I have by then." For pricing decisions: 30-day test prices — no permanent commitment.
