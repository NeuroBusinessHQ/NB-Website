What prevents employees from adopting AI?

By NeuroBusiness · Editorial review: 12 October 2026 · Evidence policy

Employees may struggle to adopt AI when its purpose is unclear, tools are inaccessible, outputs are unreliable, learning time is missing or expectations conflict. Concerns about accountability and changes to work also deserve investigation. Low use is a signal to explore, not a diagnosis of resistance.

How can companies identify AI adoption barriers?

Combine confidential feedback with observation of one real task. Ask where people stop, what they cannot access, what they must verify and whom they approach for help. Separate technical friction, skill needs, unclear policy and concerns about the work itself. Check whether managers and employees describe the same problem.

How should companies prepare employees for AI?

  1. Explain the task and the reason for changing it.
  2. Show useful examples and known failure modes using approved data.
  3. Provide protected practice time and a named support contact.
  4. Define when to check, reject or escalate an output.
  5. Invite feedback and revise the workflow based on observed difficulties.

What is human-centered AI adoption?

It is an implementation process that involves affected people in shaping work and evaluating its effects. Participation needs consequences: teams should see which concerns changed the design and which remain unresolved.

Example: training is not always the answer

If employees understand the tool but must copy information between incompatible systems, another introduction course may not remove the barrier. If users cannot distinguish plausible errors from correct outputs, task-specific practice and review support may matter more. Test the explanation against observations.

Evidence and limits

OECD Employment Outlook 2023 provides broader context on skills and AI at work. This barrier classification is a practical synthesis, not a validated NeuroBusiness scale. Do not infer barriers from personality labels or use participation data to rank employees.

Connect the findings to an adoption pilot and a readiness assessment plan.
