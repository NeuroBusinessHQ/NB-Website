What is AI readiness?

By NeuroBusiness · Editorial review: 12 October 2026 · Evidence policy

AI readiness means having the people, processes, technology and governance needed to use AI responsibly for a defined purpose. It is a judgment about a specific use case supported by evidence, not a general claim that a company is “AI-ready.”

What is an AI readiness assessment?

An assessment reviews those conditions and identifies what needs to change before a pilot or rollout. It should specify the task, affected roles, evidence collected, unresolved risks and the decision it supports. Buying licenses, attending training or expressing enthusiasm is not enough to demonstrate readiness.

Three levels to distinguish

Employee readiness concerns practical capability and support for an actual task. Leadership readiness concerns priorities, resources and accountability. Organizational readiness includes operational processes, data and tool access, review arrangements and governance. A strong score at one level does not eliminate gaps at another.

An example

Consider drafting customer replies. Employees may know how to prompt the tool, yet still lack a policy on customer information or a clear reviewer. The learning condition may be met while the data and responsibility conditions remain unresolved. A limited pilot should address those gaps before exposing real customer information.

What is not measured by a workstyle profile?

Preferences for analysis, collaboration or structure do not establish AI skill or security knowledge. NeuroBusiness’s diagnostic models can support reflection, but cannot substitute for a practical task test or a technical review.

Evidence and interpretation

NIST AI Risk Management Framework (2023) supplies risk-management context. The definition and example above are an editorial synthesis, not a standardized or validated readiness scale.
