AI readiness assessment for companies

By NeuroBusiness · Editorial review: 12 October 2026 · Evidence policy

An AI readiness assessment is a structured review of the conditions a company needs to use AI effectively and responsibly. It should identify strengths, missing evidence and barriers for a defined use case, then connect each finding to an action and an owner.

Availability: inquiry and scope discussion. A standalone NeuroBusiness AI readiness test, automated score and validated company benchmark are not currently documented as released products.

Proposed assessment process

  1. Scope: agree a workflow, affected roles, business outcome and prohibited uses.
  2. Evidence: combine interviews, a short employee questionnaire, process documentation and practical task observations.
  3. Interpretation: distinguish skill gaps from missing access, unclear rules, workload and legitimate concerns.
  4. Decision: document conditions for a limited pilot, remediation work or a pause.
  5. Review: repeat the practical checks after training and process changes.

What a useful deliverable contains

A use-case summary; readiness findings by role; an evidence register; adoption barriers; unresolved risks; a prioritized action plan; and pilot measures. The proposed format uses descriptive findings rather than a false precision score. A survey average can conceal a team with no approved tool or no time to learn.

AI readiness test versus business diagnostic

The existing NeuroBusiness 50-question diagnostic explores work patterns. The free Quick-Scan is an orientation to those patterns. Neither is presented here as an AI competency test. Companies should supplement workstyle conversations with task-based AI evaluation and technical review.

For companies in Germany

Discuss working language, employee participation, confidentiality and intended decisions before collecting responses. This page does not establish legal compliance or imply a Berlin office. Contact NeuroBusiness to clarify delivery arrangements for your team.
