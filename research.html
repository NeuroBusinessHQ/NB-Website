Research and evidence behind our AI guidance

By NeuroBusiness · Editorial review: 12 October 2026 · Evidence policy

NeuroBusiness uses external research as context for practical guidance. External evidence about AI at work does not constitute validation of the NeuroBusiness Intelligence Framework or its proposed AI readiness framework.

Risk management

NIST AI Risk Management Framework (2023) is a voluntary framework for managing AI risks. It provides a useful reference for identifying responsibilities and review processes. It is not an assessment accreditation or an endorsement of NeuroBusiness.

Work and skills

OECD Employment Outlook 2023 examines AI in the labour market, including skills and workplace implications. It supports considering work organization alongside technology; it does not establish a universal readiness score.

Workplace field evidence

Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond, Generative AI at Work, NBER Working Paper 31161 (2023) studies an AI assistant in a customer-support setting. Reported effects differ across workers. That setting cannot establish the expected benefit for every company, role or AI tool.

NeuroBusiness original work

The public methodology register documents NBIF and distinguishes proposed concepts from released functions. No peer-reviewed NeuroBusiness validation study, independent benchmark or public research dataset is claimed here.

Editorial practice

We identify the source of external findings, distinguish inference from evidence and avoid turning a study result into a product guarantee. Corrections or questions can be sent through Contact. Dates on these pages indicate editorial review, not the completion of a new study.
